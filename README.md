Analyst Memo - Project: Subscription Analytics Project

Dashboard: Subscription Analysis

Reporting Period: January 2024 December 2025

Key Insight:

-Customer Cohort Analysis: After how many months do customers typically cancel their subscriptions?

Analysis of retention across all cohorts reveals a distinct cancellation pattern. Retention remains relatively stable through month 2 at approximately 78%, then declines sharply to 50% at month 3 — a significantly steeper drop than observed at any other point in the customer lifecycle. This indicates that the largest single wave of cancellations occurs at the three-month mark.

This pattern suggests several likely drivers:

End of an initial commitment or introductory pricing period. Fitness subscriptions commonly include a three-month contract term or discounted trial rate. A substantial portion of customers appear to cancel rather than transition to full pricing once this period ends.

Churn stabilizes meaningfully after month 3. Retention declines gradually from 50% to 39% by month 9, consistent with normal ongoing attrition rather than a structural cutoff — suggesting that customers who move past the three-month mark are considerably more likely to remain long-term.

A smaller, early decline occurs between month 0 and month 1 (~15%). This aligns with typical early-stage disengagement from customers who sign up but don't use the service meaningfully.

Recommendation: Given the concentration of cancellations at month 3, we recommend investigating whether this aligns with a specific subscription plan or pricing structure, and evaluating targeted retention interventions (e.g., renewal incentives or proactive outreach) timed ahead of this critical churn point.

-Revenue Forecast: What is the expected subscription revenue over the next 12 months? Does the revenue forecast show evidence of seasonality?

Projected subscription revenue for the next 12 months (February 2026 – January 2027) totals approximately $3.33M, averaging $278K per month. The forecast reflects consistent month-over-month growth of approximately $7,775, building from the most recent actual figure of $193,614 in January 2026.

Notably, the forecast model does not appear to incorporate seasonality — it follows a near-linear trend rather than reflecting the cyclical patterns observed in the historical data. Actual revenue has shown a recurring pattern of peaking in December and declining sharply in January in both 2024 and 2025, including a ~30% drop between December 2025 and January 2026. Because the forecast does not account for this seasonal effect, projected revenue toward the end of the forecast period (particularly January 2027) may be overstated. 

We recommend treating the $3.33M estimate as an upper-bound trend projection and monitoring actual performance closely around the December–January transition.


-CAC vs. LTV: How long does it take for the 2024 and 2025 cohorts to recover their customer acquisition cost?

Both the 2024 and 2025 cohorts recover their customer acquisition cost (CAC) within approximately 12 months, indicating consistent unit economics across cohort years.

2024 cohort: Cumulative lifetime value (LTV) surpassed total CAC in December 2024, with cumulative revenue increasing from $435,806 in November to $528,892 in December — crossing the CAC threshold of approximately $528,892 at the 12-month mark (January–December 2024).

2025 cohort: A comparable pattern emerged, with cumulative LTV rising from $1,010,248 in November 2025 to $1,231,980 in December 2025, surpassing the CAC threshold of approximately $1,231,980 — again at the 12-month mark (January–December 2025).

This consistency is a positive indicator: despite total CAC increasing roughly threefold between 2024 and 2025 (from ~$529K to ~$1,232K), payback period has remained stable, suggesting that unit economics have held steady even as acquisition spend has scaled significantly.

Note: The 2025 payback figure is based on 13 months of available data and should be treated as preliminary; the estimate may be refined as additional data becomes available.
